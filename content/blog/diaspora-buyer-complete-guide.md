  ## The Legal Framework


  ### Can foreign nationals buy property in Nigeria?


  Yes. The Land Use Act of 1978 vests all land in state governors, but Nigerians
  (home or abroad) and foreign nationals can obtain statutory rights of
  occupancy. For diaspora buyers, this typically means:


  - **Certificate of Occupancy (C of O)** — for newly developed land

  - **Governor's Consent** — for transferring existing titled land

  - **Minister's Consent** — additional step for foreign nationals


  ### What documents should you receive?


  At closing, you should have:

  1. Registered Deed of Assignment

  2. Certificate of Occupancy or Governor's Consent

  3. Survey plan (redacted)

  4. Building approval (for new builds)

  5. Tax clearance certificate


  If a developer cannot produce items 2-5, walk away.


  ## Remote Closing: How It Actually Works


  ### Step 1: Virtual Tour & Inquiry

  Use 360° virtual tours to walk through properties from your phone. Book a
  video call with the sales team.


  ### Step 2: Document Verification

  Request scanned copies of title documents. Verify independently at the Lagos
  State Lands Bureau portal.


  ### Step 3: Electronic Signing

  Sign reservation forms and purchase agreements electronically using DocuSign
  or comparable platforms. Nigerian courts accept e-signatures under the
  Evidence Act 2011.


  ### Step 4: International Wire Transfer

  Pay via SWIFT wire to the developer's USD or NGN account. Nigerian banks
  accept incoming wires up to $50,000 per transaction without special
  documentation.


  ### Step 5: Power of Attorney (Optional)

  If you want someone to act on your behalf in Nigeria, execute a notarized PoA
  at the nearest Nigerian embassy or high commission.


  ## Tax Implications


  ### For Nigerian Diaspora

  - **Rental income:** 10% withholding tax (deducted at source)

  - **Capital gains on sale:** 10% CGT

  - **No inheritance tax** on Nigerian property


  ### For Foreign Nationals

  - Same as above, plus potential home-country taxes (consult your local tax
  advisor)

  - The US-UK-Nigeria tax treaties prevent double taxation in most scenarios


  ## Red Flags to Avoid


  - **"Special discount for cash payment, no receipt"** — always demand paper
  trail

  - **No registered C of O or Governor's Consent** — non-negotiable

  - **Pressure to skip legal representation** — always hire independent counsel

  - **Unrealistically high yield promises** — anything above 10% annual is
  suspicious

  - **No construction milestones in payment plan** — should be tied to physical
  progress
